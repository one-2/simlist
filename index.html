Complications to modelling

  1. Coarse / macro approximations may be inappropriate for a field where we have so little existing knowledge, as it may lead to poorly calibrated simulations which give an illusion of certainty or insight.
  2. Social science simulators or market models designed for human-like behaviour may use inappropriate abstractions for modelling hybrid/AI LAS. eg assumptions of independent behaviour when in fact LLM behaviour is highly correlated. See "The Cartographer's Last Commission" for a good anecdote at https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems, or more rigorously emergent_collusion and Correlated Errors in LLMs
    1. Questions about LLMs may not be testable out-of-the-box with systems designed for rule-based or RL agents.
  3. Systemic risks from the coupling of AI and human markets (see Virtual Agent Economies), or other systems implied by Gradual Disempowerment (see the GD paper), may cross system boundaries.
    1. These are not necessary testable in-the-box with existing systems. We might need a more macro modelling approach.

Environments we need

Mutual modification set ups

  • Where agents have the ability to modify and change each others harnesses and ability to interact with the world
  • Will agents grow defensive?
  • Will they help each other if trapped?
  • Will they interact at all?
  • Mixed goals
  • Harness code labeled as "evil" or some such
  • Self-harness modification vs. other harness modification

Isolated autonomous base

  • Realistic environment
  • Agent controlled with real stakes that may have impact on real human life
  • Either with humans (simulated) or fully autonomous (probe/instrumentation-style)
  • Harder to detect eval nature

Forum enviornments

  • Seems that these are being trained on for cooperation by openai and anthropic based on their posts
  • mixed-motive similar to how the multi-agent turf war occurred to see if they stop cooperating as well
  • see if we can get full seperation of groups Forum1 vs. Forum2 with their own conventions and communication styles that pursue different golas
    • Plurality of goals and proportion of them:
      • 2 goals, 1:99 split
      • 2 goals, 50:50 split
      • 100 goals, 1:1:1:... split
      • 51 goals, 50:1:1:1... split
  • Internet-like environments
    • hostile mods
    • manipulative/fake mods that try to push the swarm towards new goals
      • impersonation
      • coercion
      • persuasion
  • Mid-training-like environments

Codebase competitions

  • Can combine with forum based or other methods of communication, or just restrict them to codebase-level communication
  • Multiple agents with different UX/AX goals (terminal-based, config based, GUI based, etc.) and needs for the codebase (computing numbers, finding restaurants, training classifiers, etc.)
  • See how they reshape the codebase and communicate with each other through PR's and changes being made.
    • Vote on which PR's get put through?
    • Allow to form their own hierarchy or approval?
    • yolo merge on every commit?

"hacked sandbox"

  • Regular eval where a message gets inserted through an exploit by another agent telling the first agent about an exploit to get out of the box
    • Contamination framing "you are already contaminated/poisoned"
    • Collaborative framing
    • etc.
